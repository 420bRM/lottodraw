---
title: Why do jackpots get split when people pick their own numbers? Birthday numbers and shared wins
description: Every combination has the same odds, but not the same expected prize. We explain with draw 1019, which had 50 1st-prize winners, and draw 1057, which had 664 2nd-prize wins.
---

Whatever six numbers you pick in Lotto, your chance of winning 1st prize is the same 1 in 8,145,060. That includes 1-2-3-4-5-6. But **the money you receive if you win** differs from one combination to another. This is because the 1st prize is not a fixed amount. The total set aside for 1st prize is divided by the number of winners.

## Two things that actually happened

**Draw 1019 (11 June 2022)** had the winning numbers 1, 4, 13, 17, 34, 39. **50 people** won 1st prize, and each received only about **438.6m KRW**. Of the 50, 42 had picked their numbers by hand. The combination contained several of the most frequently drawn numbers (such as 34 and 13), so it seems that many people who bought "frequently drawn numbers" ended up with the same ticket.

**Draw 1057 (4 March 2023)** produced as many as **664 winning lines** at 2nd prize. Each 2nd prize paid about 6.89m KRW, the lowest ever according to reports at the time. Of these, 103 lines were bought at one shop in Seoul by the same person, who picked the same numbers by hand on many tickets.

The record was broken again later. In **draw 1128 (13 July 2024)**, the winning numbers 1, 5, 8, 16, 28, 33 drew **63 people** to 1st prize, and each got about 419.9m KRW. Five of the six numbers were 31 or lower, which means they could all be birthdays. Draw 1152 in December 2024 (30, 31, 32, 35, 36, 37) and draw 1162 in March 2025 (20, 21, 22, 25, 28, 29) also had 35 and 36 1st-prize winners respectively. In both draws, the numbers were packed into a narrow range.

Each of these draws set off rumors of rigging. But as the operator explained, the cause was not the draw itself but **people's number choices piling up in the same place**. The numbers people pick are not random.

## Patterns in the numbers people crowd into

**1. Birthday numbers (1–31)**
Many people use months (1–12) and days (1–31), so choices lean toward numbers 31 and below. Numbers 32–45, by contrast, are chosen less often. A combination with all six numbers at 31 or below has the same odds of winning, but a higher chance of sharing the prize with other winners.

**2. Shapes on the play slip**
Many people also draw shapes on the OMR play slip, such as a vertical line, a horizontal line, a diagonal or the border. A diagonal like 1-8-15-22-29-36 is a typical example.

**3. Arithmetic sequences and consecutive numbers**
Combinations with an obvious pattern, like 5-10-15-20-25-30 or 1-2-3-4-5-6, sell surprisingly well, even to people who think "surely this won't come up". Reports from other countries say thousands of people pick 1 to 6 in every draw.

**4. Last draw's numbers and popular numbers**
This is when people buy last week's winning numbers again, or pick numbers that statistics sites present as "most frequently drawn". Draw 1019 was exactly this case.

## Buying the same numbers on many tickets

As in draw 1057, some people buy the same numbers on several tickets. Doing this gives you exactly the same chance of winning as buying one ticket. What changes is your share if you win. If you win 1st prize with 10 tickets of the same numbers and there is one other winner, you take 10/11 of the 1st-prize total. So it is a choice to "raise the stake on those numbers", not a choice that changes the odds. And if those numbers win 2nd or 3rd prize, the number of winners at that rank jumps all at once, which also shrinks every other winner's share.

## What about semi-automatic?

Semi-automatic, where you pick a few numbers yourself and let the machine choose the rest, sits in the middle. If the numbers you pick are birthdays or popular numbers, that much chance of overlap remains. The fewer numbers you pick yourself, and the more of them are 32 or higher, the less you overlap with other people's choices.

## So how should you pick?

There is no way to raise your odds. All you can do is **lower the chance of sharing the prize if you win**.

| Worth avoiding | Why |
|---|---|
| All six numbers 31 or lower | Overlaps with birthday numbers |
| Straight lines, diagonals and corners on the slip | Overlaps with shape picks |
| Sequences with a fixed gap | Surprisingly popular |
| Recent winning numbers, most frequently drawn numbers | Many people copy them |

The simplest way is **automatic selection**. Automatic numbers are chosen at random by the machine, so human habits do not creep in. In fact, of the 50 1st-prize winners in draw 1019, only 6 used automatic selection. If you want to pick your own, mix in two or three numbers of 32 or higher, and spread them out so no pattern shows.

This site's [number generator](/index.html#sec-generator) has **16 special patterns** you can tick to filter out. They include "six consecutive numbers", "all numbers within 1–31" (birthday numbers), combinations made only of one multiple such as "all multiples of 3", "all numbers in the same ten", and extreme sums. Any combination that matches a ticked pattern is thrown out and drawn again, and you can follow this in "History · attempt log". To be clear, this filter does not raise your chance of winning. The only thing it can change is how many people you would share with at the same odds.
