Lotto prize tax comes down to two sentences. **Prizes of 2m KRW or less are tax-free. Above that, 22% is taken up to 300m KRW, and 33% on the part above 300m KRW.** But when you actually do the math, there are a few places where people often go wrong.

## Tax rate structure

| Prize | Tax rate | Breakdown |
|---|---|---|
| 2m KRW or less | Tax-free | — |
| Over 2m KRW up to 300m KRW | 22% | Income tax 20% + local income tax 2% |
| Portion over 300m KRW | 33% | Income tax 30% + local income tax 3% |

Under tax law, lottery winnings count as "other income". They fall under separate taxation (final withholding), which means the tax is withheld at source when the prize is paid and nothing more is owed. Winnings are not added to your other income, so the tax is the same whether your salary is high or low.

The tax-free threshold used to be 50,000 KRW, but it rose to 2m KRW in 2023. So today 4th prize (50,000 KRW) and 5th prize (5,000 KRW), and most 3rd prizes too, are paid with no tax.

## Common mistake 1: the 2m KRW is not a "deduction"

The 2m KRW is not an amount taken off your prize. It is **the threshold that decides whether tax applies at all**. If a prize is even 1 KRW over 2m KRW, 22% applies to the **full amount**, not just to the part above 2m KRW.

A 3rd prize is usually between 1.3m and 1.7m KRW, so it is tax-free. But in a draw with unusually few 3rd-prize winners, it can go over 2m KRW. For example, if 3rd prize is 2.1m KRW, 462,000 KRW is taken in tax and you receive 1,638,000 KRW. That is less than you would take home from a 1.9m KRW 3rd prize.

## Common mistake 2: 33% applies only to the part above 300m KRW

The 33% rate, on the other hand, is progressive and applies only to the part above 300m KRW. Here is the calculation for a 2.0 bn KRW 1st prize.

| Bracket | Amount | Tax rate | Tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to 300m KRW | 300m KRW | 22% | 66m KRW |
| Portion over 300m KRW | 1.7 bn KRW | 33% | 561m KRW |
| **Total** | **2.0 bn KRW** | About 31.4% | **627m KRW** |

The take-home amount is **about 1.373 bn KRW**. Strictly speaking, the tax rate is applied after subtracting the 1,000 KRW ticket price as a necessary expense. This site's calculator works it out that way too, but the difference is only a few hundred KRW.

## Take-home examples by prize rank

| Rank | Prize before tax (example) | Tax | Take-home amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st prize | 2.0 bn KRW | 627m KRW | 1.373 bn KRW |
| 1st prize | 3.0 bn KRW | 957m KRW | 2.043 bn KRW |
| 2nd prize | 60m KRW | 13.2m KRW | 46.8m KRW |
| 3rd prize | 1.5m KRW | 0 KRW | 1.5m KRW |
| 4th prize | 50,000 KRW | 0 KRW | 50,000 KRW |

The bigger the 1st prize, the closer the effective tax rate gets to 33%. At 1.0 bn KRW it is about 29.7%, and at 5.0 bn KRW about 32.3%.

## The calculation at a glance

For any amount, these three steps give you the take-home amount.

1. Check whether the prize is 2m KRW or less. If it is, the tax is 0 KRW and you are done.
2. If it is over 2m KRW, multiply the part up to 300m KRW by 22%. (If the prize is under 300m KRW, this is the whole prize × 22%.)
3. Multiply the part above 300m KRW by 33% and add it to the result of step 2.

For a 1st prize above 300m KRW, you can also get the tax in one go: "prize × 33% − 33m KRW". This works because using 22% instead of 33% on the first 300m KRW saves exactly 33m KRW (300m KRW × 11%). For 2.0 bn KRW, that is 660m KRW − 33m KRW = 627m KRW, the same result as the table above.

## How the Pension Lottery is different

The Pension Lottery 720+ 1st prize is paid as 7m KRW a month for 20 years. Tax is charged on each monthly payment separately, so no single payment goes over 300m KRW and only the 22% rate ever applies. Each month 1.54m KRW is taken off and 5.46m KRW comes in. The total is smaller than a Lotto 1st prize, but the tax structure is different because it avoids the 33% bracket.

## If you bought as a group

When a ticket bought with pooled money wins, tax is withheld at source **on the basis of the whole prize**, not each person's share. So that each person's share is not mistaken for a gift when you split the money later, it is a good idea to keep proof of the group purchase from before you buy. If one representative receives the full amount and then sends money to the others, gift tax problems can arise, so it is safer to tell the bank it is a joint win when you claim and ask for advice.
