title: How is the lotto prize decided? Sales, allocation rates and rollovers
description: Why the 1st prize ranges from a few hundred million KRW to several billion KRW from one draw to the next. We follow the steps by which ticket sales turn into prize money.
---

Both are 1st prizes, yet in some draws the prize is over 4 bn KRW and in others it stops at around 400m KRW. That is because the lotto prize is not a fixed amount set in advance; it works by **dividing up the money from tickets sold in that draw**. Let's follow the steps by which sales turn into prize money.

## Step 1: 50% of sales becomes the prize pool

Half of each draw's sales goes into the prize pool. Sales for 2025 as a whole were 6.2 trillion KRW, or about 119 bn KRW per draw on average. That makes the prize pool roughly 60 bn KRW per draw. The other half goes to the lottery fund, store commissions and operating costs. Cumulative sales from draw 1 to draw 1242 come to about 90 trillion KRW (based on press reports).

## Step 2: 4th and 5th prizes are paid first

The 4th prize (50,000 KRW) and 5th prize (5,000 KRW) are fixed amounts. The money owed to all winners at these two ranks is taken out of the prize pool first. A draw usually has more than 2 million winning games at 5th prize, so this part alone can exceed 10 bn KRW.

## Step 3: 1st, 2nd and 3rd prizes share what is left

| Rank | Share | How it is paid |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 75% of the remainder | Split equally among 1st-prize winners |
| 2nd | 12.5% of the remainder | Split equally among 2nd-prize winners |
| 3rd | 12.5% of the remainder | Split equally among 3rd-prize winners |
| 4th | Fixed | 50,000 KRW |
| 5th | Fixed | 5,000 KRW |

So the 1st prize depends on two variables: **sales in that draw** and **the number of 1st-prize winners**. Sales do not vary much from draw to draw, so what really moves the amount is the number of winners. That is why two 1st-prize winners can each receive several billion KRW, while 50 winners receive around 400m KRW each.

In a draw with an unusually large number of 5th-prize winners, less money passes to step 3, so the 1st, 2nd and 3rd prizes all shrink together. Combinations that produce many 4th and 5th-prize winners usually include numbers that many people like to pick.

## Rollovers: when there is no 1st-prize winner

If there is no 1st-prize winner at all, the 1st-prize share **rolls over to the 1st prize of the next draw**. A rollover makes the next draw's 1st-prize total bigger, and as people hear the news and buy more tickets, sales go up as well. But because a large share of the 8.15 million possible combinations get sold, rollovers are rare in Korean lotto. In draws 1–1243 there were only **14** rollovers, and most of them came in the early days (draws 1–71), when sales were low. The last rollover was in draw 463.

## Estimating the 1st prize in advance

Once you know this structure, you can roughly estimate the 1st prize before the draw. To keep the calculation simple, let's assume sales of 110 bn KRW (about 110 million games) and follow it through.

| Step | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Prize pool | 110 bn × 50% | 55 bn KRW |
| 5th prize (fixed) | 110 million games ÷ 45 × 5,000 KRW | about 12.2 bn KRW |
| 4th prize (fixed) | 110 million games ÷ 733 × 50,000 KRW | about 7.5 bn KRW |
| 1st prize total | (55 − 12.2 − 7.5) bn × 75% | about 26.5 bn KRW |
| Expected 1st-prize winners | 110 million ÷ 8,145,060 | about 13.5 |
| **1st prize per winner** | 26.5 bn ÷ 13.5 | **about 1.96 bn KRW** |

This comes out almost the same as the average 1st prize per winner (about 2.0 bn KRW). In practice, the numbers people pick are not spread evenly, so when a popular combination wins, winners pile up, and when an unpopular combination wins, one or two people take home several billion KRW.

## The range seen in past records

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Total 1st-prize winners | 10,669 |
| Average 1st prize (per winner) | about 2.012 bn KRW |
| Average 1st prize (per draw) | about 2.659 bn KRW |
| Highest per winner | Draw 19 (2003), 1 winner, about 40.72 bn KRW |
| Lowest per winner | Draw 546 (2013), 30 winners, about 406m KRW |
| Draw with the most 1st-prize winners | Draw 1128 (2024), 63 winners, about 420m KRW each |
| Draws rolled over with no 1st-prize winner | 14 |

There are two averages because of how they are calculated. If you work out the per-winner prize for each draw and then average those, early draws with huge prizes, such as draw 19 at 40.7 bn KRW, each count as one vote and pull the average up a lot. If you average across every individual winner instead, you get about 2.0 bn KRW. The figure of around 2.6 bn KRW in the example on this site's [after-tax calculator](/tax.html) is the per-draw average.

The draws with dozens of 1st-prize winners have one thing in common: the winning numbers contained patterns that people like to pick. For details, see [Why jackpots get split](/blog/why-jackpots-split.html).
