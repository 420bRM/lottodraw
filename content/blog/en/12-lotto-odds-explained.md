---
title: How small is 1 in 8.14 million? Odds by prize rank and expected value
description: We use combinations to work out where the odds for 1st through 5th prize come from, and check the expected value of a single 1,000 KRW game.
---

You have probably heard that the odds of winning the lotto's 1st prize are 1 in 8,145,060. If you work out where that number comes from, and what the odds are for 2nd through 5th prize, the structure of the game becomes much clearer.

## The total number of outcomes

The number of ways to choose 6 of 45 numbers, ignoring order, comes from the combination formula.

> ₄₅C₆ = 45×44×43×42×41×40 ÷ (6×5×4×3×2×1) = **8,145,060**

The one game you buy is one of these 8.15 million.

## Odds by prize rank

| Rank | Condition | Matching combinations | Odds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st prize | 6 matched | 1 | 1 / 8,145,060 |
| 2nd prize | 5 + bonus | 6 | 1 / 1,357,510 |
| 3rd prize | 5 matched | 228 | 1 / 35,724 |
| 4th prize | 4 matched | 11,115 | 1 / 733 |
| 5th prize | 3 matched | 182,780 | 1 / 45 |

Here is how a few of them are calculated.

- **2nd prize**: 6 ways to match 5 of the 6 winning numbers × 1 way for the last slot to be the bonus number = 6
- **3rd prize**: 6 ways to match 5 numbers × 38 ways for the last slot to be neither a winning number nor the bonus = 228
- **5th prize**: choosing 3 of the winning numbers (20 ways) × choosing 3 of the other 39 numbers (9,139 ways) = 182,780

Adding up all ranks, the chance that one game wins something is about 2.4%, or about once in 42 games. Most of those wins are the 5,000 KRW 5th prize.

## If you buy one game every week

Turning the odds into "once every so many weeks" makes them easier to grasp. These are average intervals, assuming you buy one game every week without fail.

| Rank | Average interval | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| 5th prize | 45 weeks | about 11 months |
| 4th prize | 733 weeks | about 14 years |
| 3rd prize | 35,724 weeks | about 687 years |
| 2nd prize | 1,357,510 weeks | about 26,000 years |
| 1st prize | 8,145,060 weeks | about 156,000 years |

Even if you buy one game a week for a lifetime (60 adult years, about 3,100 weeks), you would see 4th prize about four times, and your chance of ever seeing 3rd prize is about 8%. Your chance of 1st prize is about 0.04%, or one person in 2,600.

## Ways to feel 1 in 8.15 million

- If you buy 5 games (5,000 KRW) every week, it takes about **21,000 years** for your chance of 1st prize to reach 50%.
- Buying all 8.15 million games yourself would cost 8.14 bn KRW. Even then, there is no guarantee you would take 1st prize alone.
- The odds are similar to picking exactly one person at random from everyone in Seoul (about 9.3 million people).

## Expected value: what comes back when you pay 1,000 KRW

The share of lotto sales paid back as prizes is fixed at **50%**. The rest goes to the Lottery Fund (a little over 40%) and to sales commissions and operating costs. So over the long run, a 1,000 KRW game has an expected value of about **500 KRW** before tax. Once you add the tax on large prizes, the real expected value is even lower.

Even in draws where a rollover makes the 1st prize larger, this structure barely changes. A larger prize brings more buyers, and with them a higher chance of a [split jackpot](/blog/why-jackpots-split.html).

## So why do people buy?

On expected value alone, the lotto is a game where the loss is certain. Many people still buy, probably because 1,000 KRW lets them imagine "what if" for a whole week. The healthiest approach is to treat it as the cost of that daydream and enjoy it within money you can spare.
