---
title: How do you actually claim a 1st prize?
description: Where to go, what to bring, the deadline and the tax taken off. These are the actual steps from checking your ticket to seeing the money in your bank account.
---

The draw ends at 8:35 pm on Saturday, and the six numbers on the ticket in your hand match the screen. The first thing to do is surprisingly simple: **put the ticket somewhere safe and tell no one**. A Lotto ticket is a bearer security, so whoever holds it owns it. If it is lost or damaged, there is in practice no way to get it back.

## Each rank is paid at a different place

| Rank | Where to claim | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1st prize | NH NongHyup Bank head office | Jung-gu, Seoul; in person |
| 2nd and 3rd prize | NH NongHyup Bank branches nationwide | Some small sub-branches excluded |
| 4th and 5th prize | Lottery retailer or NongHyup Bank branch | Retailers pay on the spot |

Only 1st prize has to go to the head office because the amount is large and there is a separate process to protect winners. At the head office, identity checks and payment take place in a private room, and if you wish, staff can arrange wealth management advice for you. The advice is optional, and turning it down has no effect on your payment.

The same rule applies if you bought online (on the Donghaeng Lottery site). Small prizes such as 5th prize are paid automatically into your deposit balance, but a 1st prize must still be claimed in person at the NongHyup Bank head office.

## You need two things

- **The original winning ticket**: the barcode and numbers must be intact. Fold marks are fine, but if it is torn or wet and cannot be scanned, payment may be refused.
- **Your own ID**: a physical ID such as a resident registration card, driver's license or passport.

Beyond that, just bring the details of a **bank account in your own name** to receive the money. A 1st prize is not handed over in cash. It is transferred to your account. Someone else can claim on your behalf with power-of-attorney documents, but the checks take longer, so unless there is a special reason, it is quicker to go yourself.

## The deadline is one year, and one day late is too late

You must claim the prize **within one year of the payment start date (the day after the draw)**. If the last day falls on a holiday, the deadline moves to the next business day. In one real case, the winner of the 1st prize in draw 1159, drawn in February 2025 (about 1.28 bn KRW), made the news by turning up just before a deadline that had been pushed back by the Lunar New Year holiday.

Prizes not claimed by the deadline go to the Lottery Fund and are spent on public-interest projects. Several billion KRW in unclaimed prizes disappear this way every year. Prizes also earn no interest, so the later you claim, the more you lose.

## Tax is taken off before the money arrives

When you claim, the money is paid in with tax already withheld at source. 22% applies up to 300m KRW and 33% to the part above 300m KRW. This is separate taxation (final withholding), which settles the tax, so you do not need to include it in your income tax return the following year. If you win 2.0 bn KRW, you actually receive about 1.37 bn KRW. Enter the amount in the site's [after-tax calculator](/tax.html) to see it right away, and the calculation is explained in detail in [Lotto tax explained](/blog/lotto-tax-guide.html).

## How the day at the head office goes

When you tell the counter at the head office that you have a 1st-prize ticket, you are shown to a separate meeting room. Staff check by computer that the ticket is genuine and that it won, and check your ID to confirm who you are. You then fill in a payment request form, and the amount, with tax already withheld at source, is transferred to the account you name. If the paperwork is in order, it usually takes an hour or two.

Once the transfer is done, you receive guidance on what you might face after winning. It is based on what big winners have actually been through, such as a sudden rise in requests for money, rushed investments and problems at work. You do not have to decide that day how to use the money. Many experts advise keeping it in a savings deposit for at least a few months while you make a plan.

## Who to tell, and when

There is no legal duty to report or announce a win. The operator does not reveal winners' identities either. So who you tell is up to you, but big winners' stories have one thing in common. The more people you tell, the more requests you get. Many people first tell only someone they manage money with, such as a spouse, and decide about everyone else after they have claimed the prize and made a financial plan.

## Checklist before you claim

1. Take a photo of the ticket, but do not post the photo on social media or in messaging apps.
2. Keep the original ticket somewhere it cannot get wet or lost, such as a safe or a bank safe deposit box.
3. Check the draw number and your numbers again on the official Donghaeng Lottery site. Mixing up draws happens more often than you might think.
4. Bring your ID and the details of your own bank account, and visit the head office during weekday business hours.
5. Decide in advance when, and how widely, you will tell family and friends.

The process after a win is more routine than you might expect. There is no need to rush, but be sure to remember the one-year deadline.

> Where and how prizes are paid can change with the operator's policy. Before you go, we recommend checking the Donghaeng Lottery notices and NongHyup Bank's guidance once more.
